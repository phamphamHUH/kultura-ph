# Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao

_BARMM · Mindanao · Regional center: Cotabato City_

## Overview

BARMM is the heartland of Islam in the Philippines, with rich artistic traditions in dance, music, textiles and metalwork. Cultural groups include the Maguindanaon, Maranao, Tausug, Yakan, Sama and Iranun, each with its own distinct heritage. Sulu is no longer part of the region and is listed under Region IX in the PSGC as of 3Q 2026.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts: The Artistry of Identity and Tradition

**Summary:**

Bangsamoro craftsmanship reflects the identities and artistic knowledge of its diverse communities. From intricate Maranao okir designs and the symbolic Sarimanok to Yakan textiles and kulintang music, these traditions preserve cultural expressions passed down through generations.

**Detailed Story:**

The artistic traditions of the Bangsamoro region reflect the creativity, beliefs, and identities of its diverse cultural communities. Among the most recognizable expressions is **okir**, an elaborate decorative art associated particularly with the Maranao people. Characterized by flowing curves, intricate floral patterns, and geometric designs, okir is commonly applied to woodcarving, metalwork, traditional architecture, and household objects. These designs can be seen in the decorative elements of torogan houses, where craftsmanship communicates social identity, artistic achievement, and cultural heritage. Metalworking traditions also demonstrate the skill of local artisans, who transform materials into decorative and functional objects.

Another recognizable expression is the **Sarimanok**, a colorful mythical bird associated with Maranao culture. Often depicted with elaborate feathers, ornamental details, and a fish held in its beak, the Sarimanok has become an important visual representation of Maranao artistic expression. Its image appears in various decorative forms, including carvings, paintings, and textiles.

In the island communities of Basilan, **Yakan weaving** represents generations of textile-making knowledge. Yakan weavers are known for their intricate handwoven fabrics, particularly those featuring bold geometric patterns and carefully arranged colors. Traditional textiles are produced using techniques passed down through generations, with patterns and designs reflecting the community's artistic identity and weaving heritage.

Music is another important part of the region's cultural expression through **kulintang gong ensembles**. The kulintang is a set of horizontally arranged, graduated gongs played alongside other percussion instruments. Performances create layered rhythms used in social gatherings, celebrations, and cultural occasions. Together, these artistic traditions demonstrate how craftsmanship, visual symbolism, textiles, and music preserve the knowledge and identities of Bangsamoro communities.

### Food and Cuisine: Flavors Rooted in Community and Tradition

**Summary:**

Bangsamoro cuisine brings together distinctive spices, local ingredients, and traditional cooking techniques. Dishes such as Satti, Piyanggang manok, Pastil, and Maranao palapa represent the culinary identities of different communities and the role of food in everyday life and shared gatherings.

**Detailed Story:**

The cuisine of the Bangsamoro region reflects the culinary knowledge of its different communities, combining local ingredients, distinctive spices, and preparation methods developed through generations. Its dishes are closely connected to family gatherings, everyday meals, and cultural celebrations, making food an important expression of community identity.

One recognizable dish is **Satti**, particularly associated with Tausug cuisine. It consists of grilled meat, commonly served on skewers, accompanied by a thick, flavorful, and spicy sauce. It is traditionally eaten with rice shaped into small portions, making it a familiar breakfast and street-food offering in parts of the Sulu Archipelago and other areas with Tausug communities. Its combination of smoky grilled meat and richly seasoned sauce demonstrates the region's preference for bold and distinctive flavors.

Another notable dish is **Piyanggang manok**, a Tausug preparation of chicken cooked with a mixture of spices and burnt grated coconut. The coconut is toasted until dark before being incorporated into the dish, giving the sauce its characteristic deep color and smoky flavor. Combined with ingredients such as ginger, turmeric, and other seasonings, the preparation produces a rich and aromatic meal that reflects the distinctive cooking traditions of the Tausug people.

**Pastil** is a popular rice-based dish associated particularly with Maguindanaon cuisine. It consists of steamed rice topped with seasoned shredded meat, often chicken or beef, and wrapped in banana leaves. Its convenient packaging makes it suitable for everyday meals and travel, while its simple preparation demonstrates how familiar ingredients can become an important part of local food culture.

Meanwhile, **Maranao palapa** is a flavorful condiment closely associated with Maranao cooking. Traditionally prepared using pounded scallions, ginger, and chili peppers, it adds a distinctive combination of heat and aroma to various dishes. It can be incorporated into meals during cooking or served as an accompaniment. More than a seasoning, palapa represents a recognizable element of Maranao culinary identity and the community's appreciation for bold flavors.

Together, these dishes illustrate the diversity of Bangsamoro cuisine. From grilled meat and coconut-based preparations to banana-leaf-wrapped rice and aromatic condiments, each food reflects the ingredients, preferences, and culinary knowledge that communities continue to preserve and share.

### Heritage Places: Landscapes, Architecture, and Historical Connections

**Summary:**

Bangsamoro heritage places reflect the region's history, Islamic traditions, indigenous architecture, and maritime connections. From Maranao torogan houses and Lake Lanao to the Tawi-Tawi Islands and Sheikh Karimol Makhdum Mosque, these landmarks preserve the historical and cultural relationships of local communities.

**Detailed Story:**

The Bangsamoro region is home to heritage places that reflect centuries of cultural development, traditional architecture, religious history, and relationships with the natural environment. These places provide important connections to the experiences and identities of the communities that have lived in the region across generations.

Among the most significant examples of traditional architecture are the **Torogan**, the ancestral royal houses of the Maranao people. Traditionally associated with Maranao royalty and members of the upper social class, these structures are distinguished by their elevated wooden construction, large interiors, and elaborate decorative elements. Their most recognizable features include carved panolong, the projecting beams decorated with intricate okir designs. Beyond their architectural beauty, torogan houses represent traditional social organization, craftsmanship, and the cultural values of Maranao communities. They also demonstrate how architecture can communicate a community's identity and relationship with its environment.

Another important landmark is **Lake Lanao**, one of the largest lakes in the Philippines, located in Lanao del Sur. The lake holds deep significance for the Maranao people, whose name itself is associated with the lake and its surrounding communities. It has historically supported fishing, agriculture, transportation, and everyday livelihoods. Its surrounding landscape also holds cultural and spiritual importance, appearing in local traditions, stories, and the collective identity of the Maranao. Beyond its cultural significance, Lake Lanao is an important freshwater ecosystem that supports biodiversity and provides resources for surrounding communities.

The **Tawi-Tawi Islands** represent another important part of the region's cultural and natural heritage. Located in the southwestern Philippines, the island province is home to diverse maritime communities, including the Sama and Sama Dilaut. Its coastal settlements, surrounding waters, and island landscapes reflect generations of relationships with the sea. Fishing, boat-making, inter-island trade, and other maritime activities have influenced the livelihoods and cultural practices of local communities. The islands also demonstrate the region's historical connections with neighboring areas of Southeast Asia through seafaring, commerce, and cultural exchange.

A particularly significant religious landmark is the **Sheikh Karimol Makhdum Mosque** on Simunul Island, Tawi-Tawi. Traditionally recognized as the oldest mosque in the Philippines, it is associated with the arrival of Islam in the Sulu Archipelago during the fourteenth century. The mosque is connected to the tradition of Sheikh Karimol Makhdum, an Islamic missionary credited with helping introduce and spread Islam in the area. Its historical importance extends beyond its physical structure, representing the development of Islamic faith, scholarship, and community life in the southern Philippines.

Together, these heritage places reveal the many dimensions of Bangsamoro identity. From traditional royal architecture and culturally significant landscapes to maritime communities and historic religious sites, they preserve stories of faith, craftsmanship, settlement, and cultural continuity that remain meaningful to the people of the region.
