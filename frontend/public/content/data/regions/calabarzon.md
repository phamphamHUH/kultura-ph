# CALABARZON

_Region IV-A · Luzon · Regional center: Calamba, Laguna_

## Overview

Close to Manila, this region was the heartland of Tagalog culture and of the 1896 Revolution. It is known for devotional festivals, fine woodcarving and a strong tradition of artisan towns.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts: The Artistry of Identity and Tradition

**Summary:**

CALABARZON's artistic identity is shaped by generations of skilled woodcarvers, metalworkers, embroiderers, and visual artists who preserve the craftsmanship of their communities.

**Detailed Story:**

CALABARZON has a long tradition of craftsmanship, with different towns becoming known for particular forms of artistic production. Paete, Laguna, is especially recognized for its woodcarving tradition. Local artisans transform blocks of wood into religious figures, decorative sculptures, furniture, and other intricate creations. The craftsmanship requires patience, precision, and an understanding of the natural characteristics of wood. These carved works are often associated with religious devotion and traditional Filipino decorative arts, making Paete an important center of woodcarving heritage.

Taal, Batangas, is known for its traditional balisong, or butterfly knife, craftsmanship, as well as its embroidery tradition. The balisong represents the town's history of metalworking and specialized craftsmanship, while Taal embroidery showcases the careful needlework used to create intricate designs on garments and textiles. In Angono, Rizal, visual arts and papier-mâché have become important expressions of local identity. The town's artistic tradition includes paintings, sculptures, and the creation of large papier-mâché figures known as higantes. These crafts demonstrate how CALABARZON's communities preserve their identity through different forms of artistic expression, passing techniques and creative knowledge from one generation to another.

### Food and Cuisine: Flavors Rooted in Community and Tradition

**Summary:**

CALABARZON's cuisine combines traditional Tagalog dishes, locally produced beverages, dairy products, baked goods, and regional specialties that reflect the agricultural richness of its provinces.

**Detailed Story:**

The food culture of CALABARZON reflects the agricultural resources, local industries, and culinary traditions of its five provinces. Batangas is particularly associated with kapeng barako, a strong and aromatic coffee traditionally produced from Liberica coffee beans. The coffee has become an important part of Batangueño identity and is often enjoyed during family gatherings and everyday conversations. The province is also known for lomi, a hearty noodle soup characterized by thick noodles and a rich, savory broth.

Laguna contributes several well-known delicacies, particularly buko pie, a pastry filled with young coconut, and kesong puti, a soft white cheese traditionally made from carabao's milk. Quezon is recognized for pancit habhab, a noodle dish traditionally served on a banana leaf, and Lucban longganisa, a flavorful local sausage known for its distinctive garlic seasoning. Lambanog, a distilled coconut-based beverage associated with Quezon and other coconut-producing areas, also reflects the importance of coconut farming in the region. Together, these products and dishes illustrate how CALABARZON communities have developed distinctive food traditions from locally available agricultural ingredients and family recipes.

### Heritage Places: Landscapes, Architecture, and Historical Connections

**Summary:**

CALABARZON's heritage destinations preserve the region's colonial history, revolutionary past, geological formations, and natural landscapes.

**Detailed Story:**

CALABARZON is home to numerous historical and natural landmarks that reveal different chapters of Philippine history. Taal Heritage Town in Batangas preserves an architectural landscape characterized by ancestral houses, old churches, and traditional streets. Its historic buildings reflect the wealth and influence of prominent families during the Spanish colonial period. Nearby, Taal Volcano stands as one of the country's notable geological features, with its volcanic landscape and surrounding lake forming an important part of Batangas' natural identity.

In Cavite, the Aguinaldo Shrine in Kawit commemorates the country's declaration of independence on June 12, 1898. The ancestral home of Emilio Aguinaldo serves as a reminder of the Philippine Revolution and the establishment of the First Philippine Republic. In Angono, Rizal, the Angono Petroglyphs preserve prehistoric rock engravings believed to be among the oldest known examples of rock art in the Philippines. These figures provide a glimpse into the artistic expressions of early inhabitants. Meanwhile, Pagsanjan Falls in Laguna is recognized for its dramatic waterfalls and surrounding natural scenery. These destinations collectively represent the region's contribution to Philippine history, its artistic past, and its natural heritage.
