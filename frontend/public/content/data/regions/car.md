# Cordillera Administrative Region

_The Cordilleras · Luzon · Regional center: Baguio City_

## Overview

Home to many Indigenous peoples, often grouped together as Igorot, the Cordilleras are known for centuries-old rice terraces, strong community rituals and resistance to colonization. Culture here is tied closely to land, ancestors and the agricultural calendar.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts: The Artistry of Identity and Tradition

**Summary:**

Cordillera craftsmanship reflects Indigenous knowledge, ancestral beliefs, and community identity through woodcarving, handwoven textiles, traditional tattooing, and basketry.

**Detailed Story:**

The Cordillera Administrative Region is home to diverse Indigenous communities whose artistic traditions are deeply connected to their beliefs, environment, and everyday lives. Among its most recognized crafts is Ifugao woodcarving, particularly the creation of bulul figures. These carved wooden figures traditionally represent rice guardians associated with the protection of harvests and agricultural prosperity. Often placed in granaries, bulul figures are connected to rituals and ancestral beliefs, reflecting the importance of rice cultivation in Ifugao society. Their distinctive forms demonstrate the craftsmanship and spiritual traditions passed down through generations.

Handwoven Cordillera textiles are another important expression of Indigenous identity. Communities use traditional weaving techniques to create garments, blankets, and other textiles featuring geometric patterns and colors associated with their cultural backgrounds. In Kalinga, batok refers to traditional tattooing practices that carry cultural meanings connected to identity, personal experiences, and community heritage. Basketry is also widely practiced, with artisans using locally available materials to create containers and household objects suited to agricultural and everyday activities. These different forms of craftsmanship demonstrate how Cordillera communities preserve their heritage through objects and artistic practices that remain connected to their ancestral knowledge.

### Food and Cuisine: Flavors Rooted in Community and Tradition

**Summary:**

Cordillera cuisine reflects mountain living, traditional food preservation, agricultural production, and the use of locally available ingredients.

**Detailed Story:**

Food in the Cordillera region is closely associated with its mountainous environment and the agricultural practices of its Indigenous communities. Pinikpikan is one of the region's most recognized traditional chicken dishes, commonly prepared with ginger, vegetables, and other ingredients depending on the community and recipe. It is associated with traditional food preparation and communal gatherings. Etag, a preserved pork product, reflects the importance of food preservation in mountain communities. The meat is traditionally salted and cured, allowing it to be stored and used in different dishes. Kiniing, another traditional preserved meat, is prepared through smoking and is particularly associated with communities in the Mountain Province.

The region is also known for agricultural products that have become important parts of its local food identity. Benguet coffee is valued for its distinct flavor and connection to the highland coffee-growing communities. La Trinidad, Benguet, is particularly recognized for its strawberries, which are enjoyed fresh and incorporated into jams, desserts, and other products. Tapey, a traditional fermented rice beverage, is associated with Indigenous community gatherings and cultural practices, although its preparation and use vary between communities. Together, these foods reveal the Cordillera people's knowledge of agriculture, preservation, and the natural resources available in their mountainous surroundings.

### Heritage Places: Landscapes, Architecture, and Historical Connections

**Summary:**

The Cordillera's heritage places preserve the achievements of Indigenous engineering, ancestral burial traditions, mountain landscapes, and the development of Baguio as a highland city.

**Detailed Story:**

The heritage of the Cordillera Administrative Region is deeply connected to its mountainous environment and the traditions of its Indigenous peoples. The Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage property, demonstrate centuries of agricultural knowledge and community cooperation. Constructed along mountain slopes, these terraces are supported by traditional irrigation systems and carefully maintained landscapes. The Ifugao rice terraces are particularly recognized for their engineering and cultural significance, showing how communities developed sustainable ways to cultivate steep mountainous terrain.

Sagada's hanging coffins represent another distinctive cultural practice. Associated with traditional burial customs, these coffins are placed in elevated locations, including cliffs and rock formations. The practice reflects ancestral beliefs concerning death, respect for the deceased, and the relationship between the living and their ancestors. In Baguio City, Burnham Park serves as an important public space that reflects the city's development as a mountain destination and recreational center. Mt. Pulag, one of the highest mountains in the Philippines, is celebrated for its highland ecosystems, diverse plant life, and famous sea-of-clouds views. Together, these places reveal the Cordillera's combination of Indigenous heritage, historical practices, and remarkable mountain environments.
