# Ilocos Region

_Region I · Luzon · Regional center: San Fernando, La Union_

## Overview

The Ilocos coast is known for Spanish-era architecture, frugal and hardworking Ilocano culture, and a strong weaving tradition. Pangasinan, at the southern end of the region, has its own distinct language and identity.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts

**Summary:**

Abel Iloco weaving, Burnay pottery and Inabel textiles show how Ilocano communities turn practical materials into lasting works of art. These traditions connect everyday life with skills passed from one generation to the next.

**Detailed Story:**

Abel Iloco weaving is a handloom tradition associated with Ilocos, producing sturdy cloth used for garments, household pieces and other woven goods. The process requires patience and a careful arrangement of threads, with patterns and color combinations giving each piece its character. Burnay pottery of Vigan is made by shaping clay into jars and vessels, traditionally useful for storing water, food and fermented products. The jars are known for their earthy appearance and the skill required to form and fire them. Inabel textiles are woven fabrics that may feature stripes, checks and other designs. They reflect both the practical needs and artistic preferences of local households, while sustaining the knowledge of Ilocano weavers.

### Food

**Summary:**

Ilocano and Pangasinan food reflects a resourceful relationship with farms, gardens, livestock and the sea. Strong, savory flavors and methods of preserving ingredients are part of the region's well-known food identity.

**Detailed Story:**

Bagnet is pork that is boiled and then fried until the skin becomes crisp, creating a rich dish often served with vegetables or dipping sauces. Pinakbet combines local vegetables—commonly including bitter melon, eggplant and squash—with fermented fish or shrimp seasoning. Vigan longganisa is a small, garlicky sausage known for its distinct savory flavor. Ilocos empanada is a crisp rice-flour pastry commonly filled with vegetables, egg and longganisa. Dinengdeng is a vegetable dish simmered in a light broth and seasoned with bagoong, reflecting the Ilocano practice of making use of seasonal produce. Bibingka is a rice cake traditionally cooked in a lined clay pot or similar oven, often enjoyed during celebrations. Pangasinan bangus, or milkfish, is prepared in many ways—fresh, grilled, fried or marinated—and reflects the province's strong aquaculture and fishing traditions.

### Heritage Places

**Summary:**

From preserved colonial streets and churches to modern wind-energy landmarks and island landscapes, Ilocos heritage brings together architecture, engineering and the natural environment.

**Detailed Story:**

The Historic City of Vigan, recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserves a distinctive streetscape of ancestral houses, cobbled streets and buildings shaped by centuries of trade and cultural exchange. Paoay Church, part of the UNESCO-listed Baroque Churches of the Philippines, is admired for its massive buttresses and distinctive stone architecture, which demonstrate how church builders adapted European forms to local conditions. The Bangui Windmills line the coast and have become a recognizable symbol of renewable energy and the region's windswept landscape. Hundred Islands National Park in Pangasinan protects a group of small islands and islets known for their coastal scenery and marine environment. The Bantay Bell Tower in Ilocos Sur stands as a historic landmark overlooking the town and is closely associated with the area's religious and colonial-era heritage.
