# Central Luzon

_Region III · Luzon · Regional center: San Fernando, Pampanga_

## Overview

Known as the Rice Granary of the Philippines and as the culinary capital of the country, thanks to Kapampangan cooking. Bulacan played a central role in the 1898 Malolos Congress, while Aeta communities in the highlands maintain some of the oldest cultures in the archipelago.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts

**Summary:**

Central Luzon's crafts combine festive design, practical materials and skilled metalwork. From illuminated lanterns to woven hats and jewelry, these traditions express the creativity and livelihood of communities across the region.

**Detailed Story:**

Parol making in San Fernando, Pampanga is especially known for the creation of large, elaborate Christmas lanterns. Artisans work with frames, colored materials, lights and electrical components to create patterns that appear to move or change when illuminated. Baliuag buntal hats are woven from fine strips of buntal palm fiber, shaped into lightweight hats through careful preparation and weaving. The craft requires skill in producing an even texture and a well-formed brim. Bulacan gold and jewelry work includes the shaping, assembling and finishing of precious-metal ornaments. The craft reflects the technical knowledge of local makers and the role of jewelry production in the region's artisan economy.

### Food

**Summary:**

Central Luzon's cuisine is shaped by productive farms, livestock raising and a strong Kapampangan cooking tradition. Its dishes range from richly seasoned meat and fermented foods to milk-based sweets and locally grown fruit.

**Detailed Story:**

Sisig, strongly associated with Pampanga, is commonly prepared with chopped and seasoned parts of pork, cooked with onions and served hot. Tocino is sweet-cured meat, often pork, that is typically fried or grilled and served with rice. Kare-kare is a rich stew of meat and vegetables in a peanut-based sauce, traditionally served with bagoong on the side. Buro is a fermented preparation, often made with rice and fish or vegetables, valued for its distinctive sour and savory flavor. Tibok-tibok is a soft Kapampangan milk pudding traditionally made with carabao's milk and topped with latik. Pastillas de leche from Bulacan are soft milk candies made by cooking milk and sugar into a smooth, sweet confection. Zambales mangoes are prized for their sweetness and are enjoyed fresh or used in desserts and other products.

### Heritage Places

**Summary:**

Central Luzon's heritage includes places connected to Philippine nationhood, volcanic landscapes, wartime memory and the coastal history of Aurora. Together, they reveal the region's historical and natural significance.

**Detailed Story:**

Barasoain Church in Malolos, Bulacan is closely associated with the First Philippine Republic and the Malolos Congress of 1898–1899. It remains an important symbol of the country's struggle for independence and constitutional government. Mount Pinatubo, whose major 1991 eruption reshaped the surrounding landscape, is now known for its crater lake and striking volcanic terrain; it also serves as a reminder of the power of natural events and the resilience of nearby communities. Bataan Death March memorials commemorate the Filipino and American prisoners of war who endured the forced march in 1942, preserving the memory of suffering and sacrifice during World War II. Baler, Aurora is known for its coastal setting and historical associations, including the Siege of Baler and the town's role in the history of the Philippine-American period.
