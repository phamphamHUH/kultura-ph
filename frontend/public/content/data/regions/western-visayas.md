# Western Visayas

_Region VI · Visayas · Regional center: Iloilo City_

## Overview

Western Visayas is known for gracious hospitality, grand festivals with Indigenous Ati roots, and fine textile traditions. Since the creation of the Negros Island Region in 2024, Negros Occidental is no longer part of this region.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts

**Summary:**

Western Visayas is known for textile traditions and decorative work that turn locally available fibers and shells into valued products. These crafts preserve hand skills while supporting artisans and local livelihoods.

**Detailed Story:**

Piña cloth weaving in Aklan uses fibers extracted from pineapple leaves to create a fine, lightweight textile valued for its delicate appearance. Preparing the fibers and weaving them into cloth require considerable time and specialized skill. Hablon handloom weaving in Iloilo produces fabric on traditional looms, often using cotton or other fibers and a range of patterns and colors. Hablon is used for clothing, accessories and contemporary textile products, connecting older weaving knowledge with present-day design. Capiz shell crafts make use of the thin, translucent shells of windowpane oysters. Artisans cut, shape and assemble the shells into lamps, panels, décor and other objects, taking advantage of their soft glow and distinctive texture.

### Food

**Summary:**

Western Visayas brings together noodle soups, sweet delicacies, tropical fruit and seafood. Its food culture reflects Iloilo's culinary reputation and the agricultural and coastal resources of the surrounding provinces.

**Detailed Story:**

La Paz batchoy is an Iloilo noodle soup commonly served with pork, liver, broth, noodles and crunchy toppings. It is known for its rich, savory flavor and is closely associated with the La Paz district of Iloilo City. Piaya is a flat, flaky pastry filled traditionally with muscovado sugar, then cooked until lightly crisp. Guimaras mangoes are famous for their sweetness and are eaten fresh or used in juices, desserts and other products. Napoleones are layered puff pastries filled with custard and topped with a sweet glaze, a well-known delicacy associated with Negros and commonly enjoyed across the wider Western Visayas food scene. Capiz seafood includes a variety of fish, shellfish and other marine products prepared grilled, steamed, fried or in soups, reflecting the province's coastal resources.

### Heritage Places

**Summary:**

Western Visayas' heritage ranges from a UNESCO-listed church and historic religious centers to internationally known beaches and island landscapes. These places connect faith, architecture, tourism and the natural environment.

**Detailed Story:**

Miag-ao Church in Iloilo is part of the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Baroque Churches of the Philippines. Its stone façade includes detailed reliefs that combine Christian imagery with local elements, making it a distinctive example of colonial-era religious architecture. Molo Church in Iloilo City is known for its Gothic-inspired design and its prominent place in the historic Molo district. Jaro Cathedral is an important religious landmark and center of Catholic life in Iloilo, with a history closely tied to the development of the city. Boracay in Aklan is internationally known for its white-sand coastline and clear waters; its natural beauty has made it a major tourism destination and an important part of the region's contemporary identity. Guimaras is an island province known for its agricultural landscapes, beaches and coastal communities, as well as its celebrated mango production.
