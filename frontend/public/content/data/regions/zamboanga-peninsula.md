# Zamboanga Peninsula

_Region IX · Mindanao · Regional center: Pagadian City_

## Overview

Zamboanga is the only place in Asia with a Spanish-based creole language, Chavacano, spoken widely in Zamboanga City. The peninsula is home to Subanen, Sama, Tausug and Visayan-descended communities. In the Philippine Standard Geographic Code (PSGC) as of 3Q 2026, Sulu is listed under this region.

## Cultural Stories

### Crafts and Arts

**Summary:**

Zamboanga Peninsula's crafts express the maritime and cultural identities of its communities. Boat-building, Subanen textile and bead traditions, and Tausug weaving preserve skills that are closely connected to local ways of life.

**Detailed Story:**

Vinta boat-building is associated with the colorful traditional sailing boats of the Zamboanga area and the maritime communities of the region. Building a vinta requires knowledge of boat construction and the sea, while its distinctive colorful sails have become a recognizable cultural symbol. Subanen weaving and beadwork include the making of textiles and decorative items using patterns, colors and materials associated with Subanen communities. These works are connected to clothing, adornment and cultural expression, and their production helps pass traditional skills between generations. Pis syabit, a traditional Tausug textile, is a finely woven square cloth known for its intricate geometric designs and vivid colors. It may be worn as a head covering or used as a decorative textile, and its patterns demonstrate the skill and artistic vision of Tausug weavers.

### Food

**Summary:**

The peninsula's cuisine reflects its coastal setting and the many communities that live across the region. Seafood, grilled dishes, sweet refreshments and traditional Tausug preparations contribute to a distinctive food culture.

**Detailed Story:**

Curacha is a large spanner crab associated with Zamboanga, commonly steamed or cooked with rich sauces. Satti consists of grilled skewered meat served with rice cakes and a distinctive spicy sauce, making it a popular breakfast or street-food dish in the area. Knickerbocker is a chilled dessert associated with Zamboanga City, typically combining chopped fruits, gelatin and ice cream in a sweet, colorful serving. Zamboanga sardines reflect the importance of the region's fishing and canning industries, with sardines prepared and preserved in various sauces. Tinuom is a traditional dish name used for food wrapped in leaves and cooked, with local preparations varying by community. Tiyula itum, or Tausug black soup, is a deeply flavored dish whose dark color traditionally comes from charred coconut; it is commonly prepared with beef or other meat and spices.

### Heritage Places

**Summary:**

The region's heritage spans religious and colonial-era landmarks, distinctive islands, coastal destinations and places tied to national history. Its sites reflect the peninsula's maritime connections and cultural diversity.

**Detailed Story:**

Fort Pilar in Zamboanga City is a historic Spanish-era fort that later became an important Catholic shrine dedicated to Our Lady of the Pillar. It remains a major religious and historical landmark. Santa Cruz Island, known for its pink-sand beach, is recognized for its unusual shoreline color created by a mixture of white sand and fragments of red organ-pipe coral. Its coastal and marine environments are also important parts of its appeal. Dakak Beach in Dapitan is known for its coastline and clear waters, forming part of the region's northern coastal tourism landscape. The Rizal Shrine in Dapitan preserves the place where national hero José Rizal lived during his exile from 1892 to 1896, including structures and exhibits that interpret this period of his life. The Sulu Archipelago is a chain of islands with long-standing maritime connections and diverse communities, including Tausug and Sama peoples; its cultural history is closely tied to seafaring, trade and the sea.
